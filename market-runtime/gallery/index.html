Project 1

2026

A browser-based inspection tool for AI-generated 3D assets

CGTrader is the world's largest 3D model marketplace. Its AI tool generates 3D assets from a text prompt or an image, and the viewer is where artists inspect, retexture, remesh, and export them. It also loads marketplace models and user uploads, so neither the file format nor the topology can be predicted.

Role: design engineer

I built the viewer end to end as a standalone module embedded in CGTrader's website, working closely with their front-end developers, designers, and AI team. Everything on the viewer side is mine: model loading and export, quad and triangle wireframes, all view modes including the exploded view, mesh statistics, camera controls, and environment map management.

Stack: Three.js, Typescript, Zustand

WebGL and WebGPU only render triangles. A standard wireframe draws every internal diagonal, so artists see noise instead of topology. I rebuilt the quad cage: adjacent coplanar triangles are paired, their shared diagonal is discarded, and only the boundary edges are drawn. The wireframe now shows the edge flow artists expect from their 3D software. Segmented parts come either from the AI or from the model's own mesh hierarchy, so their sizes and origins are arbitrary. A fixed offset buries small parts inside large ones. I compute each offset from the part's bounding box and its position within the whole model, so spacing stays even whatever the input.

Project 2

2024

A studio website built from a single shape

W3R.ONE is a tech studio building web, mobile, blockchain, and AI products for clients such as Sephora, LVMH, and Havas. Its site had to present that range in a bilingual, multi-section narrative without reading like a slide deck. The hexagon carries the identity: seen in isometric view, a cube becomes a hexagon, and every media mask, service card, and transition is built from that shape.

Role: design engineer

I built the front end and the motion system that drives it: the isometric hero cube that shifts from SVG to a Three.js volume, the pinned split-screen scroll, team portraits masked into the cube's faces, the dark-to-light inversion on the services section, and extruded hexagon service cards rendered in WebGL.

Stack: WebGL, Next.js, GSAP

The page chains nine pinned sections, and the hexagon has to stay readable through every handoff: cube to team grid, dark to light, card to card. I drove the whole page from GSAP ScrollTrigger, so every transition is tied to scroll position and plays the same way backward as forward. The hero had to read as a flat brand mark before it read as volume. In isometric view, the cube's silhouette is the hexagon itself. I kept it in SVG through the first frames, then handed off to a Three.js mesh on the same silhouette, which keeps WebGL out of the initial paint.

Project 3

2026

Depth as input: real geometry for AI product visuals

Presti is a Y Combinator-backed platform that generates product visuals at scale for over 400 retailers, marketplaces, and manufacturers. Brands stage a single product across many environments without a shoot for each. The composer sits upstream of generation: users assemble a 3D scene, its render passes guide the image model, and the product keeps its real geometry. The 3D scene is what stops a product from being redrawn into something the brand never made.

Role: design engineer

I built the 3D layer from scratch inside Presti's existing Next.js app: the scene graph, catalog GLB loading, and a custom gizmo system for move, rotate, and scale, with multi-select and duplication. I also built the image output pipeline that feeds the generation: a depth pass, one segmentation pass per object, and a full scene render.

Stack: WebGL, Next.js, Zustand

Depth drives the generation: any drift in near and far planes or in framing changes the geometry the model returns. I normalized depth against the scene bounds and locked every pass to a single camera. Products come back in their real proportions, not reinterpreted. Users are not 3D artists, so placement had to be forgiving. Every transform snaps to a grid and to fixed angles, a drop-to-surface raycast lands each object on the nearest geometry below it, and collision resolution on insert keeps a new model from spawning inside another. A scene comes together in a few drags.

Project 4

2024

Five countries, five audiences, one narrative in motion

Grandir is an early-childhood group founded in France in 2000, running over 1,000 nurseries and preschools across five countries and welcoming more than 48,000 children every day. Grandir.com is the group site, speaking at once to parents, corporate clients, candidates, and investors. The challenge is editorial: five audiences, five markets, and one narrative that has to stay readable while it moves.

Role: design engineer

Working from static mockups, I built the site's entire motion layer in vanilla, on top of another developer's integration. I wrote every scroll timeline in GSAP and ScrollTrigger: pinned sections, staggered image reveals, count-ups on key figures, the dotted world map and its arcs, and a city carousel driven by scroll position.

Stack: GSAP, Typescript, Vite

Integration and motion were built in parallel by two developers, and the site had to behave the same in every browser. I kept the animation layer self-contained, with GSAP and ScrollTrigger as the single source of truth for scroll state. Each section runs its own timeline bound to its own trigger, so markup and motion could evolve independently without either side blocking the other. The mockups were static, so the motion was designed directly in the browser. I set every timing live, then rebuilt each sequence for mobile rather than scaling the desktop version down.

Project 5

2023

A 3D island as the spine of a studio's story

Reaali is a French studio that builds geolocated augmented reality games for cultural and tourist sites, including Assassin's Creed experiences at Les Invalides and the Louvre-Lens. Their site turns a single 3D scene, a floating island carrying a bonsai and ruins, into the spine of the scroll narrative. As the reader scrolls, the camera travels around the island while the studio's technologies, figures, and references unfold.

Role: design engineer

I integrated and animated the whole site from a static design file. I wrote the Three.js scene, the scroll timeline, and every section transition, driving the camera, the SVG trail, and the masked video from a single scroll progress value. Before writing any animation, I rebuilt the 3D asset for a mobile budget.

Stack: WebGL, GSAP, Blender

The island arrived as a production mesh, sized for offline rendering. I cut geometry and reworked the texture set until the whole scene fit in a 376 KB file, keeping the leaf density and the silhouette intact. Phones and desktop load the same asset, with no stripped-down mobile variant. The SVG trail anchors to DOM elements and had to stay aligned at every viewport width. I recompute the path from measured element positions on resize instead of hardcoding coordinates, and drive its stroke from the same ScrollTrigger progress as the camera.
